I was thinking about new material that I will use for teaching this coming semester (starting the third week of February) and suddenly compiled the following list of links:

- William Briggs writes [It is time to stop teaching Frequentism to non-statisticians](http://arxiv.org/abs/1201.2590) in a paper submitted to The American Statistician. Clearly he doesn’t want to be controversial with an abstract that reads *We should cease teaching frequentist statistics to undergraduates and switch to Bayes. Doing so will reduce the amount of confusion and over-certainty rife among users of statistics.*
- Making the most of Google searches or a [simple graphical explanation of search options](http://9gag.com/gag/1836348) for students doing online research. HT: Rafael Maia. I have to pass this link to our students doing Research Methods.
- Reading newspapers or other sources of news is often a frustrating endeavor for the scientifically minded person. Tom Scott makes the experience more bearable with his handy design for [journalism warning labels](http://www.tomscott.com/warnings/). This would be the perfect complement to [Stats Chat’s](http://www.statschat.org.nz/) “Stat of the week” competition.
- Some honesty in statistics: [footnote in a statistics textbook](http://i.imgur.com/17Ua5.jpg). HT: Vince Buffalo.
- [R-bloggers](http://www.r-bloggers.com/), the aggregator of bloggers writing about R, has reached 300 bloggers. Quantum Forest is part of that ever-growing R orgy.
- I’m ordering a couple of copies of his book [R in Action](https://www.librarything.com/work/9913076) for our department.
- If you have to sell R to your colleagues, David Smith of Revolutions fame has good news: [the popularity of R as a language has increased](http://blog.revolutionanalytics.com/2012/01/r-jumps-from-25-to-19-in-tiobe-rankings.html), overtaking both SAS and Matlab. Here are the [TIOBE rankings](http://www.tiobe.com/index.php/content/paperinfo/tpci/index.html).
- Currently re-reading: [Experimental Design and Data Analysis for Biologists](https://www.librarything.com/work/435574) by Gerry Quinn and Mick Keough. It would be nice to have R code for the whole book; please let me know in the comments if you have seen it somewhere in the internets.
- P.S. Douglas Andrews reminds us about [The big mistake: teaching stat as though it were math](http://community.amstat.org/blogs/blogviewer/?BlogKey=784e55b4-cb6c-499a-a2da-391e548d36b2). This commentary does link with Briggs’s rant, but it also smells of professional protectionism.

Enough procrastination. Let’s keep on filling out [PBRF](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance_Based_Research_Fund) forms; it is the right time for that hexennial activity.
