This coming Monday we start the first semester in [Canterbury](http://www.canterbury.ac.nz/) (and in New Zealand for that matter). We are all looking forward to an earthquake-free year; more realistically, I’d be happy with low magnitude aftershocks.

- The Wall Street Journal [reports](http://online.wsj.com/article/SB10001424052970203315804577209230884246636.html) that more pediatricians are ‘firing’ patients that refuse to use vaccines. I’m wondering about practices that will cluster with ‘vaccine refusers’.
- I am collaborating with [a researcher](https://profiles.canterbury.ac.nz/Michael-Hayes) in Electrical Engineering where he and his students develop very cool tools for us (see example in this [previous post](/2011/10/26/longitudinal-analysis-autocorrelation-makes-a-difference/) on dealing with autocorrelation in mixed models). They use Python to control the tools, data extraction and do some basic processing (isn’t that cool?). Python + Scipy have moved a lot towards creating a nice environment for scientific computing; however, in my opinion setting an R environment is way easier than dealing with all the versions for python, wxpython, etc. At the end I went for the 32 bit version in OS X because 64 bit is not supported (wxpython targets carbon, meh).
- [Why not publish your data too?](http://wicherts.socsci.uva.nl/). I’ll be uploading datasets that I’ve used in my papers (at least the ones that do not contain industry/confidential data).
- Timandra Harkness asks Have you been seduced by statistics? in [Significance Journal](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1740-9713.2012.00549.x/pdf) (free access). In the same issue Matt Briggs asks another important question [Why do statisticians answer silly questions that no one ever asks?](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1740-9713.2012.00542.x/pdf)
- [How companies learn your secrets:](http://www.nytimes.com/2012/02/19/magazine/shopping-habits.html?_r=2&hp=&pagewanted=all) the creepy side of statistics/machine learning.
- Feeling all smug in a discussion in Engineering: people struggling with software licensing (Office, Matlab, etc). I pointed out that we didn’t have any licensing issues with R to be at the bleeding edge when teaching. Take that!
- In the nothing-to-do with-stats category, <del>Retronaut</del> Open Culture displays a beautiful collection of[ Soviet space propaganda posters](https://www.openculture.com/2019/08/the-glorious-poster-art-of-the-soviet-space-program-in-its-golden-age-1958-1963.html). Changed the destination because the site disappeared.
