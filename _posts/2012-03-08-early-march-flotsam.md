It has been a strange last ten days since we unexpectedly entered grant writing mode. I was looking forward to work on this issue near the end of the year but a likely change on funding agency priorities requires applying in a few weeks; unfortunately, it means that all this is happening at the same time I am teaching.

- As usual I got involved in a strange, for me, project which will require semantic analysis of international treaties. I will start having a look at Latent Semantic Analysis using [lsa](http://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/lsa/index.html) in R and [gensim](http://pypi.python.org/pypi/gensim) in Python. I’ll have to retrieve documents from the web and process them in quite a few ways.
- The success of some of Hadley Wickham’s packages got me thinking about underlying design issues in R that make functions so hard to master for users. Don’t get me wrong, I still think that R is great, but why are there so many problems to understand part of the core functionality? A quick web search will highlight that there is, for example, an incredible amount of confusion on how to use the `apply` family of functions. The management of dates and strings is also a sore point. I perfectly understand the need for, and even the desirability of, having new packages that extend the functionality of R. However, this is another kettle of fish; we are talking about making sane design choices so there is no need to repackage *basic* functionality to make it usable.
- Talking about failures, Andrew Gelman mentions the sempiternal problem of designers [Turn(ing) a Boring Bar Graph into a 3D Masterpiece](http://andrewgelman.com/2012/02/turn-a-boring-bar-graph-into-a-3d-masterpiece/) or, as a commenter put it, “Turn(ing) a Boring Bar Graph into a 3D Pile of Steaming Crap”. While it is always easy to have a laugh on designers, we should remember that the abundance of 3D piles\[…\] also reflects our failure to make the point on good data presentation clear. Well, that and <span style="text-decoration: line-through;">the spawn of evil</span> Microsoft Excel and PowerPoint.
- A cool collection of [movie snippets](http://www.math.harvard.edu/~knill/mathmovies/) that display mathematics.
- [http://numfocus.org](http://numfocus.org/) a foundation for supporting scientific computing in Python.
- Andrew Gelman again, this time pointing out to Thaddeus Tarpey’s presentation [All models are right… most are useless](http://andrewgelman.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/03/tarpey.pdf) (PDF), focusing on the positive aspects of model approximation.

And that’s all folks.
